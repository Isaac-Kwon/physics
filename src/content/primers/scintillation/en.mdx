Some materials, when a charged particle excites their atoms or molecules, give the energy back as a faint flash of visible or ultraviolet light. This is **scintillation**. Plastics, crystals such as sodium iodide, and liquid noble gases such as argon and xenon all do it. The amount of light is proportional to the energy deposited, typically of order 10,000 photons per MeV.

A flash of a few thousand photons still has to become an electrical pulse.

- A **photomultiplier tube** (PMT) frees one electron from a coating for each photon, then multiplies it through a chain of electrodes, about a million times.
- A **silicon photomultiplier** (SiPM) is a grid of tiny silicon diodes, each of which fires when a single photon arrives. It is compact and works inside magnetic fields.

Both can count single photons, so both can measure faint, fast light.
