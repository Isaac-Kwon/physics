title: Telling particles apart

Pions, kaons and protons all leave similar tracks. What separates them is **mass**, which a detector cannot weigh directly. Instead it measures two things and combines them.

- **Momentum** $p$ from the bending in a magnetic field.
- **Speed** $\beta$ from one of several methods: the energy loss dE/dx (which depends on $\beta$), the flight time over a known distance, or the angle of Cherenkov light.

Then

$$m = \frac{p}{\beta\gamma c}.$$

At the same momentum, a heavier particle is slower. Plot dE/dx against momentum, and each particle type falls on its own band. Where bands cross, the method fails at that momentum, and another one takes over.

Identification is often statistical: each track gets a probability for each type.
