Protons and neutrons are not elementary. Each is made of three **quarks** (up and down types) bound together by **gluons**, the carriers of the strong force.

The strong force acts on a property called colour charge. Its theory is quantum chromodynamics (QCD). Two features matter here.

- **Confinement.** The force between quarks does not fade with distance. Pull two apart and the energy in the field grows until it creates a new quark–antiquark pair. Quarks are never seen alone, only inside composite particles called hadrons.
- **Freedom at high energy.** At very short distances, or very high temperature and density, the force becomes weaker and quarks and gluons can move over distances much larger than a proton. That state is the quark–gluon plasma.

Most of a proton's mass comes from the energy of this field, not from the quark masses.
