Cosmic rays hit the top of the atmosphere and make **muons**, heavy cousins of the electron. At the surface about one muon crosses each square centimetre every minute. Muons go through almost anything, and they can mimic or hide the rare events that dark-matter and neutrino experiments look for.

Rock stops them. Depth is quoted in **metres of water equivalent** (m.w.e.): the thickness of water that would absorb the same amount. Under 1 to 2 kilometres of rock, about 3000 to 6000 m.w.e., the muon flux falls by a factor of a million or more.

Two more things matter underground:

- **Radiopurity.** Trace uranium, thorium and potassium in every material decay and make background. Parts are chosen and cleaned for it.
- **Shielding.** Water, lead, copper, or the detector medium itself absorbs what is left.
