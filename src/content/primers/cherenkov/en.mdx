title: Cherenkov light

Nothing travels faster than light in vacuum. In water, glass or gas, however, light slows down to $c/n$, where $n$ is the refractive index. A fast particle can then outrun the light in that material.

When it does, the material responds like air to a supersonic jet: a cone-shaped wavefront of light, the **Cherenkov radiation**. The light is emitted at a fixed angle to the particle's path, with

$$\cos\theta_c = \frac{1}{n\beta},\qquad \beta = v/c.$$

Two things follow. There is a speed threshold, $\beta > 1/n$, below which no light appears. Above it, the ring's opening angle reveals the speed. Together with a momentum measurement, the speed tells you the particle's mass.
