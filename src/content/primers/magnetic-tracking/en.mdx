A charged particle in a magnetic field $B$ moves on a circle (or a helix, if it also moves along the field). The magnetic force bends the path without changing the speed.

Two things can be read from the curve:

- **Direction of bending**: the sign of the electric charge.
- **Radius of curvature $R$**: the momentum, since

$$p\,[\text{GeV}/c] \approx 0.3\; B\,[\text{T}]\; R\,[\text{m}]$$

(for a particle with one unit of charge, and $p$ the momentum perpendicular to the field).

A stiffer, higher-momentum track curves less. A detector that records several points along the path, a tracker, can fit the circle and obtain both charge and momentum. A stronger field bends more, so it measures momentum better over a given length.
